## My story

I can't trace my life to some remarkable event that shaped my long-term goals or who I am today. Maybe part of it was growing up as the baby of the family and never being taken seriously. Maybe it was watching sparring matches from the sidelines at Taekwondo practice. Maybe it was every time I've had a burning question and no idea how to answer it. Maybe even the ongoing struggle to balance my West African heritage with my life in the United States. Maybe it was none of these things.

I've always perceived myself as one with every interest, desire, and challenge I encounter rather than a summation of individual components. The parts of me that enjoy baking, drawing, and comics are equally fascinated by the brain all of its capabilities. The me that's fascinated by traditional hair styling techniques finds the same artistic beauty watching machine learning programs come to life. So what is it that drives me?

At my core, I've always grappled with the idea of wanting to do something, knowing how, and having every capability to, but being prevented from it by factors outside of your control. This concept of lost agency has informed every one of my pursuits since high school, from my education to my career. 

In the lab, I want to improve an amputee's quality of life, researching factors in their rehabilitation and using them to design prostheses that integrate more smoothly with the living bodies. Outside, I want to create ways for underrepresented scholars to carve their own paths. I'm dedicating my career to inclusion and my time at Tech has reminded me why. Here, I've met people with fascinating stories to tell, each of which deserves to be highlighted. They prove to me the world is far too vast for one not to think beyond oneself, which is why I choose to give back.
