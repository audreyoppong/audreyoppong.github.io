## My story

I can't trace my life to some remarkable event that shaped my long-term goals or who I am today. Maybe part of it was growing up as the baby of the family and never being taken seriously. Maybe it was watching sparring matches from the sidelines at Taekwondo practice. Maybe it was every time I've had a burning question and no idea how to answer it. Maybe even the ongoing struggle to balance my West African heritage with my life in the United States. Maybe it was none of these things.

I've always perceived myself as one with every interest, desire, and challenge I encounter, rather than a summation of individual components. The parts of me that enjoy baking and comics are equally fascinated by the brain and all of its capabilities. The me that's fascinated by hair modeling and fashion finds the same artistic beauty watching machine learning programs come to life. So what is it that drives me?

Looking back, those moments share a common thread: I've always grappled with the idea of wanting to do something, knowing how, and having every ability to, but being prevented from it by factors outside of my control. This concept of lost agency has informed every one of my pursuits since high school, from my education to my career. 

In the lab, I want to improve an amputee's quality of life, researching factors in their rehabilitation and using them to design prostheses that integrate with the human body to restore sensation and control. Outside, I want to create ways for underrepresented scholars to carve their own paths by teaching, mentoring, and advocating. I'm dedicating my career to inclusion and my time at Tech has reminded me why. Here, I've met people with fascinating stories to tell, each of which deserves to be highlighted. They show me that potential is everywhere, but access isn't. If my work removes even a fraction of the barriers standing in someone else's way, that is how I choose to use my own agency.
